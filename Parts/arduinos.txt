================================================================
  01 - MICROCONTROLLERS (THE BRAINS)
================================================================

We use TWO Arduino Uno R3 boards, each running its own sketch.
They communicate over a serial wire (see 08_Communication.txt).


MASTER ARDUINO UNO R3 - "The Driver"
------------------------------------
This board is in charge of anything that has to react fast:
driving the tank and avoiding obstacles.

  * Handles drive motors (via the motor driver shield)
  * Reads the front + scanning ultrasonic sensors
  * Reads the IR distance sensor
  * Reads the MPU-6050 (accelerometer/gyro)
  * Drives the pan/tilt servo arm
  * Receives commands from the IR remote
  * STREAMS motion data out to the slave for logging/display

Why it gets the "fast" stuff: all the safety-critical decisions
(when to stop, when to turn) happen here so there's no delay
waiting on another chip.


SLAVE ARDUINO UNO R3 - "The Recorder"
-------------------------------------
This board handles slower, less time-critical jobs:

  * Drives the 16x2 LCD over I2C
  * Reads the photoresistor (light level)
  * Reads the TMP36 (temperature)
  * Reads its own ultrasonic sensor on the side shoulder
  * Sends "OBSTACLE" alerts to the master when something gets
    too close on the side
  * Receives MPU data from the master and shows it on the LCD

Powered SEPARATELY by a 9V battery per the rubric requirement.


COMMON SPECS (both boards)
--------------------------
  * Chip:        ATmega328P
  * Clock:       16 MHz
  * Flash:       32 KB
  * SRAM:        2 KB
  * Logic:       5 V
  * Digital pins: 14 (6 PWM-capable)
  * Analog pins:  6 (10-bit, reads 0-1023)


WHY TWO ARDUINOS?
-----------------
The stock Conqueror kit uses ONE Arduino. We ran out of pins
once we tried to add the IMU, multiple ultrasonics, the servo
arm, AND the LCD. Splitting into "navigator + recorder" freed
pins on both boards and let each sketch stay focused on one
job.

The trade-off: now we have to manage inter-board communication.
That's handled by the UART link — see 08_Communication.txt.
