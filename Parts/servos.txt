================================================================
  03 - SERVOS & SCANNING ARM
================================================================

The pan/tilt arm at the front of the tank holds the scanning
ultrasonic sensor + the IR distance sensor. Two micro servos
let us aim that arm.

Both servos belong to the MASTER Arduino.


2x SG90-CLASS MICRO SERVOS
--------------------------
These are the classic blue hobby servos you've seen in every
beginner robot kit.

KEY SPECS:
  * Operating voltage: 4.8 - 6 V
  * Stall torque: ~1.8 kg-cm  (plenty for the tiny arm)
  * Travel: 0 - 180 degrees
  * Speed: ~0.1 sec per 60 degrees (no load)
  * Control signal: 50 Hz PWM, 1-2 ms pulse width
    (We don't care about this - the Arduino Servo library
    handles all the PWM details. We just call servo.write(45)
    and it goes to 45 degrees.)


HOW THEY'RE MOUNTED
-------------------
  * PAN servo  -> rotates the arm left/right horizontally
  * TILT servo -> rotates the arm up/down vertically

Together they form a 2-axis aiming arm. The scanning ultrasonic
and IR sensor ride on the very tip.


PINS USED ON MASTER
-------------------
  D10 -> Pan servo control wire
  D11 -> Tilt servo control wire


CALIBRATED ANGLES
-----------------
We figured these out by hand to fit our physical mount:

    Pan center:       50 degrees
    Pan full left:   110 degrees
    Pan full right:    0 degrees
    Tilt level:      160 degrees   (sensors looking forward)


WHY WE NEED A SCANNING ARM AT ALL
---------------------------------
A single fixed forward sensor can only answer "is there
something in front of me?" That's enough to STOP, but not
enough to decide which way to TURN.

With the pan-mounted arm, after the tank stops we sweep the
sensor left and right, take distance readings at each angle,
and pick the direction with the most open space. Smarter
behavior at the cost of one servo sweep per obstacle event
(about 1-2 seconds of "thinking").
