================================================================
  04 - DISTANCE SENSORS (HOW THE TANK "SEES" OBSTACLES)
================================================================

We use 4 sensors total to detect obstacles:
  * 3x HC-SR04 ultrasonics
  * 1x Sharp GP2Y0A41 infrared distance sensor

The reason for using two DIFFERENT sensor types is fusion:
each catches things the other one misses (more on that below).


HC-SR04 ULTRASONIC SENSORS (3 total)
====================================
Classic 4-pin "googly eyes" sensor. Sends out a 40 kHz sound
pulse, listens for the echo, measures round-trip time.

COMMON SPECS (all three are identical):
  * Range:      2 cm - 400 cm
  * Accuracy:   +/- 3 mm
  * Beam:       ~15 degree cone
  * Output:     ECHO pin pulse width = round trip time
  * Distance formula:  cm = pulse_microseconds / 58


--- ULTRASONIC #1 - FRONT EYE (on the master) ---
  Fixed pointing straight forward.
  Job: detect obstacles directly ahead -> trigger a stop.
  PINS:  D13 = TRIG,  D12 = ECHO


--- ULTRASONIC #2 - SCANNING EYE (on the master) ---
  Mounted on the pan/tilt arm (see 03_Servos_and_Scanning_Arm).
  Job: after the tank stops, sweep left/right to find which
       direction has more clearance.
  PINS:  A1 = TRIG,  A2 = ECHO


--- ULTRASONIC #3 - SIDE EYE (on the slave) ---
  Mounted on the opposite shoulder from the scanning arm.
  Job: watches sideways. When something gets within 25 cm,
       the slave sends "OBSTACLE" to the master, and the
       master veers ~60 degrees away.
  PINS:  D8 = TRIG,  D7 = ECHO


SHARP GP2Y0A41SK0F - INFRARED DISTANCE SENSOR
=============================================
Short-range IR sensor. Lives on the scanning arm next to
ultrasonic #2 (master side).

KEY SPECS:
  * Range:       4 cm - 30 cm  (short-range version)
  * Output:      analog voltage (NON-linear curve)
  * Voltage:     4.5 - 5.5 V supply
  * Voltage curve (rough):
      ~3.0 V at  4 cm
      ~1.0 V at 20 cm
      ~0.4 V at 30 cm

PIN ON MASTER:  A0  (analog read)


WHY BOTH ULTRASONIC + IR?
=========================
They have OPPOSITE blind spots, which is exactly what we want.

  * ULTRASONIC = wide cone, long range. Bounces beautifully
    off walls. But it SKIPS PAST thin or absorbent objects
    like chair legs, fabric, sweatshirts.

  * IR = narrow beam, short range. Catches thin nearby
    objects. But useless past 30 cm.

By checking BOTH on the scanning arm, a side is considered
"blocked" if EITHER sensor flags an obstacle. That fusion is
what makes the tank reliable around real-world furniture
instead of just empty hallways.
